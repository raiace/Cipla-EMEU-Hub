Section 02 · Daily Life

Daily Life in Spain

The small things nobody briefed you on — including the Sunday shutdown and why you must always ask for the bill.

🛒 Groceries

  • Mercadona — best value, excellent quality, everywhere in Spain
  • Carrefour (large hypermarkets), Lidl & Aldi (budget, great quality)
  • El Corte Inglés Supermercado — premium, open Sundays
  • Indian groceries → Lavapiés (Madrid), El Raval (Barcelona)
  • Online: Mercadona.es, Carrefour.es — set up in Week 1

📱 SIM & Banking

  • SIM: Orange, Vodafone, or Movistar — bring passport. Plans from €15/month.
  • eSIM available if your phone supports it — useful for short stays
  • Banking: Revolut or N26 — open before travel, zero fees, works immediately
  • Longer stays: CaixaBank or BBVA — open with passport + NIE
  • Bizum — Spain's free instant payment app. Set up via Spanish bank if staying 1 month+.

🧹 Domestic Life

  • Domestic help ('asistenta'): €12–18/hour via agencies or TaskRabbit Spain
  • Less common than India — more self-service culture here
  • Serviced apartments: Weekly cleaning typically included
  • Laundromats (lavanderías): Available in all major cities

📅 Sunday & Siesta Reality

  • Sunday = family and rest. Most small shops and many restaurants close.
  • El Corte Inglés and some Carrefour stay open — plan groceries Saturday
  • Siesta: A cultural concept, not modern business practice — offices open all day
  • Business calls on Sunday are not expected — respect the boundary
  • Healthcare basics → see Housing section for full detail

Section 04 · Housing

Where to Live — and the Green Cross

How to find accommodation, what to expect from Spanish healthcare, and what to do when things go wrong at midnight.

🏨 Best option: Serviced apartment

  • Fully furnished, bills included, flexible term — typically €1,800–3,500/month for 1BR
  • Madrid: Salamanca, Chamberí, Retiro, Justicia — safe, central, well-connected
  • Barcelona: Eixample, Sarrià-Sant Gervasi, Gràcia — safe, expat-friendly
  • Cipla HR typically arranges and confirms short-stay accommodation

💡 Short Stay Tips

  • Download Google Maps and Cabify before arrival
  • Nearest Mercadona or Carrefour for groceries — set up online delivery Week 1
  • Keep HR contact saved — they manage booking logistics
  • Card payments accepted almost everywhere — cash rarely needed
⚠️ July–August: Book at least 8 weeks early or expect a very difficult search. This is Spain's peak holiday and tourist season — accommodation demand is at its highest, prices spike, and quality properties in Madrid and Barcelona are taken fast. Many landlords raise rents during this period. If your assignment falls in or near August, confirm housing with Cipla HR well before departure.

🏠 Private Rental

  • Competitive market — quality apartments go quickly in both cities
  • NIE required for leases of 3 months+ — HR Global Mobility assists (apply 2–3 weeks before arrival)
  • Deposit: Typically 2 months rent + first month upfront
  • Furnished apartments often offer flexible 3–6 month contracts
  • Platforms: Idealista, Fotocasa, Spotahome

💼 HR Global Mobility

  • NIE registration support — contact 2–3 weeks before arrival
  • Lease negotiation and landlord liaison
  • Tax, visa, and banking setup support
  • Always contact HR before starting your own housing search

Healthcare in Spain

🏥 Private — Seguro Médico (use this)

  • Cipla provides private insurance — confirm network (Sanitas, Adeslas, or Mapfre) with HR before travel
  • Hospital Clínica Quirónsalud: Best private network, English-speaking, recommended
  • Use Sanitas/Adeslas app to book same-day appointments
  • Always carry your insurance card — without it you pay upfront and claim later
  • Indian medicines: Bring 3-month supply — Spanish brand names differ

🟢 Farmacia — Your First Stop

  • The green cross (farmacia) is everywhere in Spain — and pharmacists are excellent
  • Pharmacists can advise and prescribe some medications — brilliant for minor issues, no appointment needed
  • Farmacia de guardia: 24-hour emergency pharmacy — any closed farmacia lists the nearest open one on their door
  • Urgencias (A&E): Any hospital must treat emergencies regardless of insurance status
  • Emergency: 112 for all emergencies — works anywhere in the EU

Safety in Spain

✅ Spain is generally very safe

  • Violent crime against visitors is rare — main risk is pickpocketing
  • Night safety: Cities are safe after dark — outdoor social life continues late
  • Use Cabify or licensed taxi after midnight in unfamiliar areas
  • Normal urban caution applies in busy tourist areas

⚠️ Pickpocket hotspots

  • Las Ramblas (Barcelona), Sagrada Família queue, La Boqueria market
  • Puerta del Sol and Gran Vía (Madrid), Metro Line 1 tourist routes
  • Prevention: Money belt or front pocket. Backpack in front in crowds.
  • Scams: Friendship bracelet on Las Ramblas, shell game near tourist spots
Section 08 · Customs & Language

Social Customs & Spanish Essentials

You don't need to be fluent. Even 'hola, gracias, la cuenta, ¡Salud!' earns immediate warmth. Spanish is phonetic — what you see is what you say.

01 · Norm

Dos besos — two cheek kisses

The standard Spanish greeting among people who know each other — left cheek first. In business, handshakes on first meeting. Over time, as trust builds, physical greetings become warmer. Lean in and engage warmly — pulling back reads as coldness.

02 · Norm

Spain is loud — and that's engagement

Spanish social settings are genuinely loud. Everyone talking at once, interrupting, laughing across the table — this is engagement, not chaos. In meetings, people talking over each other during debate shows they are interested. It is not rudeness.

03 · Norm

Regionalism is real and matters deeply

A Catalan in Barcelona, a Basque in Bilbao, and an Andalusian in Seville have genuinely different cultures. Acknowledge regional identity — never dismiss it or confuse it with being 'just Spanish'. Asking about their city is always a great conversation starter.

Section 09 · Work Culture

The Spanish Workplace

Relationship-driven, hierarchy-aware, and lunch-serious. Adjust your calendar — and your expectations — before your first meeting.

01 · Core Principle

The long lunch is a business meeting

Business lunches in Spain last 1.5–2.5 hours. Eating quickly and returning to your desk signals you are not investing in the relationship. If a Spanish colleague invites you to lunch — stay for the full meal, including dessert and coffee.

02 · Core Principle

Hierarchy exists — relationships cut across it

Spanish organisations have clear hierarchy. But genuine trust with someone unlocks their support regardless of position. Invest in the relationship first; professional results follow naturally. Personal warmth is the foundation of everything.

03 · Core Principle

Time is flexible — professionally and socially

Business meetings may start 5–10 minutes late. Social events: 20–30 minutes late. Dinner at 9pm means 9:30pm. This is cultural rhythm. Arriving exactly on time for a Spanish dinner makes you the first guest by 30 minutes.

For Indian Professionals — Read This Carefully

More relaxed formality than India — first-name basis in most Spanish companies from Day 1
Never book a meeting 2pm–4pm — this is the lunch window. Move it if you already have one.
Debate and push-back in meetings is healthy and welcomed — not disrespectful
WhatsApp used for informal follow-up; email is primary for formal communication
Networking is everything — the people who get things done in Spain are the ones who know everyone
In Barcelona: acknowledge Catalan culture warmly — ask about the city, not just Spain
August (1–20): Do not plan any critical decisions, contract signings, or approvals — Spain is on vacation

"In Spain, the relationship always comes before the transaction. Build the personal connection first — and the professional results will follow, reliably and warmly."

🔐 GDPR & Data Privacy — Applies From Day 1

Spain operates under EU GDPR + LOPDGDD (Ley Orgánica 3/2018)

As an EU member state, Spain follows the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). Spain's national LOPDGDD adds further obligations around employee data and digital rights. These rules apply to you as a Cipla professional from the moment you arrive.

  • → Recording any meeting or call requires explicit consent from all participants
  • → WhatsApp for work is restricted in many Spanish companies — use Teams or Slack as directed by Cipla IT
  • → Colleague personal data (salary, health, family) must never be shared without consent
  • → Do not store work-related EU personal data on personal devices
  • → Any suspected data breach must be reported to Cipla IT within 72 hours (GDPR requirement)
