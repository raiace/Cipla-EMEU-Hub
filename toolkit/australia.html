Section 01 · Country Snapshot

Australia at a Glance

26 million people across the world's 6th largest landmass. Everyone lives near the coast. The seasons are reversed. The emergency number is 000. Start here.

000

Australia's emergency number is 000 — Triple Zero. Not 999. Not 112. Police, Ambulance, and Fire are all reached on 000. This is different from every other country in this toolkit. Save it before you read anything else.

Section 02 · Daily Life

Daily Life in Australia

Almost entirely cashless. Open on Sundays. With wildlife in suburban gardens. And a bottle-return system that gives you cash back.

🛒 Groceries

  • Woolworths ('Woolies') and Coles — everywhere, excellent quality, open 7am–midnight including Sundays
  • Aldi — outstanding quality at 20–30% lower prices
  • Harris Farm Markets (NSW), weekend farmers markets — excellent fresh produce
  • Indian/Asian groceries: Parramatta (Sydney), Clayton/Dandenong (Melbourne), Sunnybank (Brisbane), Adelaide Central Market area
  • Online: Woolworths and Coles offer same-day delivery. Uber Eats and DoorDash also deliver groceries for convenience items.
  • Sundays: Open — full hours in most locations

💳 Cashless Living

  • Tap-and-pay (EFTPOS/contactless) used for virtually every transaction — cafés, taxis, supermarkets
  • Apple Pay and Google Pay widely accepted — many Australians never carry physical cash
  • Some businesses are legally permitted to refuse cash — always carry a card
  • Osko: Near-instant free bank transfers between Australian accounts
  • Carry A$50 cash only for farmers markets or very small rural shops

📱 SIM & Connectivity

  • Buy at Woolworths, Coles, 7-Eleven, or carrier store — bring passport
  • Telstra: Best nationwide coverage — essential if travelling outside major cities
  • Optus and Vodafone: Good value in metropolitan areas
  • eSIM: Available from Telstra and Optus — activate at airport if supported
  • Broadband: NBN included in most serviced apartments. Fast and reliable.

🏥 Healthcare Basics

  • Indian visitors on business visas are NOT eligible for Medicare
  • Cipla provides private health insurance — confirm details with HR before departure
  • Pharmacies everywhere — pharmacists advise on minor issues without appointment
  • Indian medicines: Bring a 3-month supply — Australian brand names differ
  • Full healthcare detail → see Housing section

🦘 Wildlife Awareness — Practical, Not Exotic

The risk is manageable with basic awareness. Most encounters happen in suburban gardens, coastal areas, and parks.

🕷️ Redback Spider

Common in gardens, garages, outdoor furniture. Red stripe on black. Look before reaching into dark corners. If bitten: call 000 immediately.

🐍 Snakes

Present in parks, bushland, and suburban gardens. If you see one: stand still or back away slowly. Never attempt to move or handle it.

🦈 Sharks

Present in all Australian coastal waters. Swim at patrolled beaches only. Check the Surf Life Saving Australia app for alerts and beach status.

🪼 Jellyfish

Box jellyfish in northern waters (QLD, NT) are genuinely dangerous. In Sydney and Adelaide: standard caution. Swim at patrolled beaches only in northern regions.

🌊 Beach Rips

Rip currents cause more drownings than all wildlife combined. ALWAYS swim between the red and yellow flags — no exceptions, even for strong swimmers.

☀️ UV Radiation

UV 11–13 in summer. Skin damage in 11 minutes. SPF 50+, hat, shade 10am–3pm. This is medical guidance — not cultural.

  • Brisbane: CBD, South Brisbane, Fortitude Valley

Healthcare in Australia

🏥 Private Insurance — Essential

  • Indian nationals on business visas are NOT eligible for Medicare — private insurance is essential
  • Cipla provides private health insurance — confirm insurer, policy number, and coverage with HR before departure
  • Book a GP via HotDoc or HealthEngine — same/next-day slots available
  • Specialists require a GP referral — book GP first for any non-emergency concern
  • For genuine emergencies: All hospitals treat you regardless of insurance status — go to Emergency Department
  • After-hours: Call 13SICK (13 74 25) — a GP visits you at home within 1–2 hours. Available to short-stay visitors too — confirm your insurance covers the call-out fee.

💊 Pharmacies in Australia

  • Pharmacies are everywhere — look for Priceline, Terry White, Chemist Warehouse, and local independents
  • Pharmacists advise and dispense — helpful for minor issues without appointment
  • Buy SPF 50+ sunscreen at any pharmacy before you leave the airport area — Day 1 purchase
  • 24-hour pharmacies available near most addresses — search online for nearest
  • Indian medicines: Bring a 3-month supply — Australian brand names and formulations differ

Section 08 · Customs & Culture

Social Customs, Slang & Sport

When cricket comes up, mention the India-Australia series. It will make you three new friends before lunch is over.

⚠️ A Common Misconception Worth Addressing

Australia is often assumed to be a straightforward Western country — culturally, it is not. While Australia shares English language and democratic institutions with other Western nations, its cultural norms differ in important ways that matter day to day.

Alcohol is not central to professional culture here — many colleagues drink little or nothing, and nobody is expected to drink at work events. Australians are not automatically informal or immediately open in all contexts — professional boundaries exist and trust is built over time. Respect is earned through how you treat people regardless of their rank or background, not through seniority or credentials. These distinctions are real, and assuming otherwise leads to missteps.

01 · Cultural Literacy

Acknowledgement of Country

You will hear an 'Acknowledgement of Country' at the opening of professional meetings, conferences, and formal events — a brief recognition of the Traditional Custodians of the land. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples have lived here for over 60,000 years. Listen respectfully. Understanding what it is and why it matters is essential cultural literacy for working in Australia.

02 · Social Norm

Egalitarianism — everyone at the table

From Day 1 you are on first-name terms with everyone — senior leadership to the reception desk. Titles and formal address are almost never used internally. This is a deeply held Australian value — every person has equal worth regardless of rank. Your contribution is expected and welcome at all levels from the start.

03 · Community

Multicultural and genuinely welcoming

30% of Australia's population was born overseas. Major cities are genuinely diverse in daily life. Your background, accent, and cultural perspective are unremarkable and often positively interesting. The Indian-Australian community is large, well-established, and very active. Australia's diversity is part of its national identity — not just a talking point.

Section 09 · Work Culture

The Australian Workplace

Flat, egalitarian, direct with warmth, and powered by flat whites. First names at all levels from Day 1. Contribution matters more than seniority.

01 · Core Value

Egalitarianism — everyone is equal at the table

From Day 1 you are on first-name terms with everyone — senior leadership, intern, receptionist. This is not informality — it is a deeply held Australian value that every person has equal worth. Your contribution is expected and welcome from the start. Let your work do the talking, and attribute success to the team.

02 · Social Norm

Understatement is valued — let work speak

Australians value modesty. Self-promotion sits uncomfortably in professional settings. The correct tone is understated and matter-of-fact. Self-deprecating humour is a social skill. Describe results factually, attribute success to the team, and let others draw the conclusion about your contribution.

03 · Communication

Directness with warmth and humour

Australian communication is direct — if a colleague thinks your proposal needs work, they will say so clearly, but with warmth and often humour. Sarcasm and irony are widely used and are signs of comfort. A colleague who lightly teases you is signalling that they like you. Engage warmly and do not take banter personally.

04 · Balance Rule

Work-life balance is genuinely observed

Overtime is not celebrated. After-hours emails are sent but responses are not expected until the next working day. Weekends are for family, outdoors, and sport. Annual leave is 4 weeks by law — Australians take all of it. Business slows dramatically from December 23 to January 15.

The Acknowledgement

You're attending a company conference. The emcee opens with: "I'd like to begin by acknowledging the Traditional Custodians of this land, the Gadigal people of the Eora Nation, and pay my respects to their Elders past, present and emerging." You are unsure what this is. What do you do?
✅ Answer: B is correct

The Acknowledgement of Country is a sincere recognition of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples — who have lived on this land for over 60,000 years. It opens nearly all formal events in Australia. Listening respectfully is the correct response. Option A is culturally tone-deaf — this is not a warm-up. Option C is well-intentioned but excessive — the room typically responds with quiet acknowledgement, not applause.

Section 10 · Golden Rules

Australia's Golden Rules

The sun is dangerous, the coffee is serious, and everyone is on first names. Get these three right and everything else follows naturally.

✅ DO — Always
Use first names with everyone from Day 1 — including senior leaders
Wear SPF 50+ sunscreen every time you go outdoors
Learn your coffee order — a flat white signals cultural awareness
Save 000 (Triple Zero) before anything else
Swim only between the red and yellow flags at all beaches
Get your city's transit card on Day 1 — Opal, Myki, Go Card, or Metrocard
Accept BBQ and social invitations — always bring something
Confirm private health insurance details with HR before you land
Register at smartraveller.gov.au before you travel
Check the AUD exchange rate before you travel
❌ DON'T — Avoid
Never call 999 or 112 — Australia's emergency number is 000
Never use formal titles at work — 'Sir' or 'Madam' is jarring
Never self-promote loudly — describe results factually and let your work speak
Never swim outside the red and yellow flags — ever, for any reason
Never go outdoors in summer without sunscreen — UV damage is medical
Never tip on top of a Sunday or public holiday surcharge already on the bill
Never plan major business Dec 23 – Jan 15 — Australia shuts down
Never talk during an Acknowledgement of Country at meetings or events
Never underestimate distance — Perth to Sydney is a 5-hour flight
Never interact with unfamiliar wildlife — call your building manager
🏄

Before you do anything else on Day 1: Save 000. Buy SPF 50+ at the pharmacy near the airport. Get your city's transit card. Order a flat white. Say 'g'day' to your neighbour. Register at smartraveller.gov.au. None of this is difficult — it is just different.
