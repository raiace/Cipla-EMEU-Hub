Section 02 · Daily Life

Daily Life in Germany

The Sunday law, the recycling system, and the pedestrian light rules catch almost every first-time visitor. Here's what to expect.

🛒 Groceries

  • Rewe (everyday premium), Edeka (quality-focused), Kaufland (large format)
  • Aldi & Lidl (both German) — outstanding budget value
  • Alnatura / Basic — organic chains, popular in Munich
  • Indian groceries → Frankfurt near Hauptbahnhof; Munich Schwabing/Neuperlach; Berlin Neukölln
  • Online: Rewe Lieferservice, Picnic — set up Week 1 as Sunday backup
  • Hours: Mon–Sat 7am–10pm. Closed Sunday by law — no exceptions.

📅 Sonntagsruhe — The Sunday Rule

  • Almost ALL shops closed by law on Sunday — strictly observed throughout Germany
  • Open Sundays: Petrol stations (limited groceries), bakeries (until noon), restaurants
  • Closed Sundays: All supermarkets, pharmacies (except Notapotheke), most services
  • Plan your weekly groceries on Saturday — no exceptions, no workarounds
  • Ruhezeit: No drilling, loud music, or appliances after 10pm or before 7am — any day
  • Your neighbours take Ruhezeit seriously. Act accordingly from Day 1.

📱 SIM & Banking

  • SIM: Buy Day 1 at Telekom, Vodafone, or O2 — bring passport. Telekom has best rural coverage.
  • eSIM: Available from Telekom and Congstar — set up before arrival if phone supports it
  • Banking: Open N26 online before travel — works immediately. Revolut also works.
  • Longer stays: Deutsche Bank or Commerzbank require a local address — takes 2–4 weeks

🏥 Healthcare Basics

  • Germany has mandatory health insurance — you are always covered by law
  • Register with a Hausarzt (GP) in Week 1 — not when you are ill
  • Apotheke (red A cross): Pharmacists advise and dispense — more knowledgeable than UK chemists
  • Indian medicines: Bring 3-month supply — German formulations and brand names differ
  • Full healthcare detail → see Housing section

📋 Anmeldung — Mandatory Registration

  • Legally required for stays over 3 months — register at local Bürgeramt within 2 weeks of arrival
  • You receive a Meldebescheinigung (registration cert) — needed for bank accounts and some rentals
  • Book Bürgeramt appointment online in advance — they fill up weeks ahead in Munich and Frankfurt
  • HR Global Mobility team will assist — do not attempt this alone initially

🚦 Unwritten Rules — Read This

  • Never cross a red pedestrian light — even on an empty road. Germans will say something.
  • Greet bakery/pharmacy staff with 'Guten Morgen' — entering silently is considered rude
  • At supermarket checkout: pack bags fast and have payment ready — checkout speed is expected
  • Formal letters in German → translate immediately via Google Translate. Never ignore them.

German food culture is more functional than social — meals are efficient, generous, and the biggest surprise is that dinner is often cold bread.

🍺 Beer culture

  • Pils, Weißbier/Weizen (very Bavarian), Helles (Bavarian pale), Dunkel (dark lager)
  • Prost! with eye contact — both eyes — looking away while clinking is bad luck
  • Non-drinkers: Spezi, Apfelschorle, or alkoholfreies Bier — all normal and respected
  • Tip by rounding up when handing cash — say the total you want to pay, not the tip amount

🥗 Vegetarian & dietary

  • Vegetarian: Germany is good — Berlin especially has outstanding vegan culture
  • Indian food: Frankfurt (Sachsenhausen), Munich (Schwabing), Berlin (Prenzlauer Berg)
  • Halal: Widely available — especially near Turkish communities. Döner shops are almost universally halal.
  • Pork: Central to traditional German cuisine — confirm in traditional restaurants

Section 04 · Housing

Where to Live & the Red Cross Pharmacy

The Apotheke (red A) solves most non-emergency health issues. Germany's healthcare is mandatory and employer-provided. Know both before you land.

🏨 Serviced apartment (recommended)

  • Fully furnished, utilities included. Limehome, Staycity, and Adagio are reliable chains.
  • Munich: Schwabing, Maxvorstadt, Bogenhausen — safe, metro access
  • Frankfurt: Westend, Nordend, Sachsenhausen — safe, close to business
  • Cipla HR typically arranges short-stay accommodation

🏩 Hotels

  • Marriott, Hilton, Steigenberger, and Dorint (German premium chains) — all reliable and well-located
  • Download DB Navigator and Google Maps before arrival
  • Carry €50 in cash from Day 1 — many local shops are cash only
⚠️ Munich Sep–Oct (Oktoberfest): Prices 3–5× normal. Near-zero availability. If your assignment falls in or near Oktoberfest (mid-September to first Sunday of October), contact Cipla HR at least 10–12 weeks before arrival. Hotels and serviced apartments are fully booked and prices are extreme. July–August also sees high demand due to summer student intake across all major German cities.

🏠 Private Rental

  • Munich has one of the most competitive rental markets in Europe — start 4–6 weeks early with HR
  • Never transfer money without physically viewing — Munich scam listings exist
  • Kaution (deposit): Typically 2–3 months rent, held in a separate account
  • Always confirm Warmmiete (total incl. utilities) vs Kaltmiete (rent only)
  • WG (Wohngemeinschaft): Shared flat — common, affordable, good for meeting locals

💼 HR Global Mobility

  • Essential for Munich — use vetted HR contacts only
  • Anmeldung support (mandatory address registration for 3+ month stays)
  • Visa, tax equalization, and lease support
  • Contact 4–6 weeks before arrival — never leave housing to the last minute

Healthcare in Germany

🏥 GKV — Statutory Health Insurance

  • Health insurance is mandatory by law — you are always covered. Employer pays ~50% of premium.
  • Providers: TK (Techniker Krankenkasse), AOK, Barmer — all comprehensive
  • Register with a Hausarzt (GP) in Week 1 — they are your gateway to specialist care
  • Without a GP referral, many specialists will not see you
  • Always carry your Krankenversicherungskarte (health card)
  • Confirm GKV or PKV scope with HR before travel

💊 Apotheke — the Red A Pharmacy

  • Qualified pharmacists advise and dispense — significantly more capable than a UK chemist
  • Many OTC medications from India require a Rezept (prescription) in Germany — bring your supply
  • Notapotheke: 24-hour — closed Apotheken list the nearest open one on their door
  • Indian medicines: Bring 3-month supply — German brand names and formulations differ

Section 09 · Work Culture

The German Workplace

Germany has the most structured professional culture in the EMEU region. The rules are real, widely followed, and matter significantly to your professional credibility from Day 1.

01 · Non-negotiable

Punctuality — arrive 2 minutes early

Being late without calling ahead is considered disrespectful, not merely inconvenient. This applies at every level. For a German colleague, your punctuality is a direct signal of your professionalism. Call ahead even for a 5-minute delay.

02 · Core Principle

Direct communication — no subtext

If a German says 'that will not work', they mean it. This is not rudeness — it is efficiency and respect. Equally, praise means what it says — no false positivity in German professional culture. Be clear, be specific, say what you mean.

03 · Sie vs Du

Formal address — start with Sie

German has two forms of 'you': Sie (formal) and du (informal). Always start with Sie. When a colleague invites du, it is a meaningful social transition. Using du too early is presumptuous — it signals cultural unawareness.

04 · Culture Rule

Work and life are strictly separated

Working late is not dedication in Germany — it is poor planning. Emails after 6pm are frowned upon. Germany has strong employee protection laws and the culture around them is taken seriously at every level of every organisation.

For Indian Professionals — Read This Carefully

Meetings have fixed agendas sent in advance — read them before attending. Come with data.
No small talk before business — brief pleasantries, then straight to the agenda
Titles matter — 'Herr/Frau Doktor Müller' not 'Mr/Ms Müller' if they hold a doctorate
Decisions take time — German business values consensus, thoroughness, and documented decisions
Never cancel a meeting at short notice — a significant breach of professional trust
After-hours emails signal poor time management, not dedication — follow your team's norms
Disagreement in meetings is healthy — Germans debate and challenge openly and directly

"My German manager left the office at 5:01pm every day. He was also the most productive person I have ever worked with. These two facts are directly related."

🔐 GDPR & Data Privacy — Applies From Day 1

Germany operates under EU GDPR + BDSG (Bundesdatenschutzgesetz)

Cipla's Munich base operates under EU GDPR and Germany's national data protection law (BDSG), which adds additional restrictions — particularly around employee data. Germany has one of the strictest enforcement records in the EU. Data privacy is not a compliance box to tick; it is a deeply held cultural value.

Key rules that apply to you as a Cipla professional on assignment:

  • Recording any meeting or call requires explicit consent from all participants — this is legally required
  • WhatsApp is restricted or prohibited in many German companies for work communications due to data residency concerns — use Microsoft Teams or Slack as directed by Cipla IT
  • Colleague personal data (salary, health, family situation) must never be discussed or forwarded without consent
  • Do not store work-related personal data of EU colleagues on personal devices
  • Follow Cipla IT's data handling guidelines strictly — these exist to protect both you and the company
  • Any suspected data breach or privacy incident must be reported to Cipla IT within 72 hours (GDPR requirement)
